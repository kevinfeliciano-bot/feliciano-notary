2.1 Notarial Act — Definition

A "notarial act" is any official act performed by a California Notary Public. Each signature line requiring a notarial certificate counts as one notarial act. Common notarial acts in California include:

  • Acknowledgment — The signer personally appears before the Notary and declares they signed the document voluntarily and for its stated purpose.
  • Jurat — The signer personally appears, signs in the Notary's presence, and swears or affirms under oath that the contents of the document are true.
  • Certified Copy of Power of Attorney — The Notary certifies that a copy of a Power of Attorney is a true and correct copy of the original.
  • Copy Certification by Document Custodian — The custodian of a document personally appears, swears or affirms under oath that a copy is a true and accurate reproduction of the original document, and signs the affidavit in the Notary's presence. This act is used for documents that California law does not permit the Notary to certify directly. The Notary notarizes the custodian's signature on the affidavit — the Notary does not certify the underlying document.

The Client is responsible for knowing which notarial act their document requires. The Notary does not determine, advise on, or select the appropriate notarial act for any document. If you are unsure which act your document requires, please consult the document's issuing authority, preparer, or a licensed California attorney prior to your appointment.

3. Cancellation, Rescheduling & Waiting Time

3.1 Client Cancellation: The Client may cancel or reschedule an appointment with at least 24 hours' notice at no charge. Cancellations made with less than 24 hours' notice will be subject to a flat $35.00 cancellation fee.

3.2 Notary Cancellation: The Notary will provide as much advance notice as possible in the event of a necessary cancellation and will make reasonable efforts to reschedule at a mutually convenient time.

3.3 Waiting Time: If all signers are not present and ready at the scheduled appointment time, the Notary will attempt to contact the Client by both phone call and text message at the 10-minute and 15-minute marks past the scheduled time, with the time of each attempt documented. Following a 5-minute grace period after the 15-minute contact attempt, if the appointment still cannot commence, the appointment will be treated as a no-show. The Notary is not obligated to wait beyond 20 minutes past the scheduled appointment time.

3.4 No-Show: If the Client is not present and ready at the designated location within the waiting time described in Section 3.3, a flat $35.00 no-show fee will be charged, and the appointment may be rescheduled at the Client's request.

The Notary provides mobile services within a 25-mile driving radius of Fremont City Hall (3300 Capitol Ave, Fremont, CA 94538), which includes both the standard service area (up to 15 miles) and the extended service area (15–25 miles) described in Section 2.3. Travel requests beyond 25 miles are subject to availability and the additional mileage fee described in Section 2.3.

5. Identity Verification Requirements

All signers must present satisfactory evidence of identity as required under California Civil Code § 1185. Acceptable forms of identification include, but are not limited to:

  • A California driver's license or identification card issued by the DMV
  • A United States passport or passport card
  • A foreign passport
  • A U.S. military identification card
  • A valid consular identification document
  • A tribal identification card issued by a federally recognized tribal government
  • Any other form of identification constituting satisfactory evidence of identity as defined under California Civil Code § 1185

All identification must be current or have been issued within the past five years. Temporary licenses and temporary identification cards are not acceptable under any circumstances. If a signer cannot produce acceptable identification, the notarial act cannot be performed and the applicable travel fee will remain due.

6. Notarial Journal & Record Keeping

The Notary is required by California law to maintain an official notarial journal of all notarial acts performed. The journal is the exclusive property of the Notary and is confidential. The Notary will record each notarial act in the journal as required under California Government Code § 8206. Clients may request a copy of a journal entry for a specific transaction; however, the Notary reserves the right to redact information pertaining to other parties or transactions as required by law.

For any Power of Attorney document, California law also requires the signer to place their right thumbprint in the Notary's journal (California Government Code § 8206(a)(2)(G)). If the right thumb is unavailable, the left thumb or another available finger will be used and noted in the journal.

7. Remote Online Notarization (RON)

This Agreement governs in-person mobile notarization services performed using traditional paper-based methods. Remote Online Notarization (RON) — in which the signer and Notary interact via audio-visual technology without being physically present together — is not currently available in California. The Notary does not perform RON services. Clients seeking remote notarization should be aware that no California-commissioned notary is currently authorized to perform RON under California law.
