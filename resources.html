Official California Notary Forms

California law requires specific, state-prescribed wording for the two most common notarial certificates. The California Secretary of State publishes these as free PDF downloads. You do not need to print these yourself — Kevin will bring the correct certificate to your appointment — but they are provided here so you can see what each looks like.

  • Certificate of Acknowledgment — Used when a signer confirms they signed a document voluntarily (common for deeds, powers of attorney, and trust documents). California Secretary of State — Acknowledgments
  • Jurat — Used when a signer must swear or affirm, under oath, that a document’s contents are true, and sign in the notary’s presence (common for affidavits and sworn statements). California Secretary of State — Jurats
  • All notary forms, services & fees — The full list of forms published by the Secretary of State. California Secretary of State — Forms

Common Documents Clients Bring

These are documents people often ask to have notarized. Where California provides an official or statutory source, it is linked below. For documents that have no single official form, the notarization simply requires the completed document plus the appropriate notarial certificate, which Kevin provides.

  • Advance Health Care Directive — California’s statutory form appears in Probate Code §4701. You may use this form or any other valid form. California Probate Code §4701 — Statutory Form
  • Power of Attorney — Powers of attorney are typically acknowledged. There are many valid formats depending on your purpose; an attorney or the requesting institution can tell you which to use.
  • Travel Consent (minor traveling) — A private letter authorizing a minor’s travel. California has no official statutory form; the airline, destination country, or requesting party usually specifies what it must contain. Bring your completed letter and Kevin will notarize the signature.
  • Affidavits & Sworn Statements — Usually completed with a jurat (see above). Bring the finished document; do not sign it until you are in front of the notary.

Before Your Appointment

  • Have your document complete and ready, but do not sign it in advance if it requires a jurat — you must sign in the notary’s presence.
  • Bring valid, current government-issued photo identification for every signer.
  • Make sure you know which notarial act your document requires. If it isn’t marked, ask the party who gave you the document, or a licensed attorney.
  • All signers must be physically present and able to communicate directly with the notary.
