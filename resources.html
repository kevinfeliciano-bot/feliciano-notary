Official Forms

California Notary Certificates

California law requires state-prescribed wording for these certificates. Kevin brings the correct one to your appointment — these are here so you know what to expect.

Acknowledgment

Confirms a signer signed voluntarily — common for deeds, powers of attorney, and trusts.

Jurat

Signer swears or affirms, under oath, that the contents are true and signs in the notary’s presence — common for affidavits.

Common Documents

What People Bring to Notarize

Where California provides an official source, it’s linked. Otherwise, just bring your completed document — Kevin supplies the notarial certificate.

Advance Health Care Directive

California’s statutory form is in Probate Code §4701. You may use it or any other valid form.

View statutory form →

Power of Attorney

Typically acknowledged. Many valid formats exist — your attorney or the requesting institution can tell you which to use.

Travel Consent

A private letter authorizing a minor’s travel. No official California form — the airline or requesting party specifies the contents. Bring your letter and Kevin notarizes the signature.

Affidavits & Sworn Statements

Usually completed with a jurat. Bring the finished document, but don’t sign it until you’re in front of the notary.

Get Ready

Before Your Appointment

A little preparation keeps your appointment quick and smooth.

  • Have your document complete and ready — but don’t sign in advance if it needs a jurat.
  • Bring current, government-issued photo ID for every signer.
  • Know which notarial act your document requires. If it isn’t marked, ask the party who gave it to you or an attorney.
  • All signers must be physically present and able to communicate directly with the notary.
