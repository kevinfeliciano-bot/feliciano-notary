Resources

Helpful links to official California forms and information, so you can arrive at your appointment prepared.

Please read first. These links point to official California government sources, for your convenience only. As a Notary Public, Kevin cannot prepare your documents, choose which form or notarial act you need, or advise you how to complete them — that would be the unauthorized practice of law. If you’re unsure what you need, consult the document’s issuing authority or a licensed California attorney.

Official Forms

California Notary Certificates

California law requires state-prescribed wording for these certificates. Kevin brings the correct one to your appointment — these are here so you know what to expect.

Acknowledgment

Confirms a signer signed voluntarily — common for deeds, powers of attorney, and trusts.

View official form →

Jurat

Signer swears or affirms, under oath, that the contents are true and signs in the notary’s presence — common for affidavits.

Common Documents

What People Bring to Notarize

Where California provides an official source, it’s linked. Otherwise, just bring your completed document — Kevin supplies the notarial certificate.

Advance Health Care Directive

California’s official fillable Advance Health Care Directive form, per the Probate Code, from the Attorney General’s office.

Download fillable form →

Power of Attorney

Typically acknowledged. Many valid formats exist — your attorney or the requesting institution can tell you which to use.

Travel Consent

A private letter authorizing a minor’s travel. No official California form — the airline or requesting party specifies the contents. Bring your letter and Kevin notarizes the signature.

Passport Consent (Minor)

When one parent can’t appear for a child’s U.S. passport application, the absent parent notarizes Form DS-3053. Leave the signature blank until you’re with the notary; the consent expires 90 days after notarization.

Download DS-3053 →

Affidavits & Sworn Statements

Usually completed with a jurat. Bring the finished document, but don’t sign it until you’re in front of the notary.

Declaration of Domestic Partnership

California’s Declaration of Domestic Partnership (Form DP-1) must be signed by both partners with a notarized certificate of acknowledgment before filing with the Secretary of State.

Download DP-1 →

Vehicle Title Replacement / Transfer

The DMV’s Application for Replacement or Transfer of Title (REG 227). If the vehicle has a legal owner/lienholder, the release section must be notarized.

Download REG 227 →

Lien Satisfied / Title Holder Release

The DMV’s Lien Satisfied/Legal Owner/Title Holder Release (REG 166). Must be notarized when submitted with an application for a duplicate California title.

Download REG 166 →

Certified Copy Requests

Requesting a certified copy of a California birth, death, or marriage record requires a sworn statement on the application that must be notarized — that’s where Kevin comes in. (Informational copies don’t require notarization.) Download the official CDPH application for your record type below.

Get Ready

Before Your Appointment

A little preparation keeps your appointment quick and smooth.

  • Have your document complete and ready — but don’t sign in advance if it needs a jurat.
  • Bring current, government-issued photo ID for every signer.
  • Know which notarial act your document requires. If it isn’t marked, ask the party who gave it to you or an attorney.
  • All signers must be physically present and able to communicate directly with the notary.
